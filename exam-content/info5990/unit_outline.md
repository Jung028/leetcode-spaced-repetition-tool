# INFO5990 (NE) — Unit Outline

Source: [Canvas — INFO5990 (NE)](https://canvas.sydney.edu.au/courses/73752)

## Unit Introduction

Welcome!

This Unit of Study introduces the students to the nature of professional practice in information technology within business environments. It will explore relevant concepts, standards, techniques, and professional issues, including aspects such as interpersonal and organisational communication, human resources and conflict resolution, professional ethics, social impacts of IT, data security, data quality assurance, system audit, investigative research and project management practices. Practical and real-world case studies will be used as part of the learning to enhance the in-class teachings to meet the needs of the industry.

For more initial information, see the welcome announcement on Ed Discussions (access via the link in the left-hand menu).

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit of study you should be able to:

| # | Objective | DL |
|---|-----------|----|
| 1 | Understand issues relating to ethics and professional responsibility in the IT profession and be able to analyze and resolve ethical dilemmas. | DL2, DL1 |
| 2 | Understand issues related to software testing, data security, data quality, and quality assurance in the IT industry, and learn methods to solve those issues using IT project management | DL2, DL1 |
| 3 | Analyse wider issues and problems concerning professional practice in IT, and learn current trends in human resource management in the IT industry. | DL2, DL1 |
| 4 | Understand the use of IT in different industries and evaluate the impact of information technology on individuals and organizations within our globalized world. | DL2, DL1 |
| 5 | Demonstrate an ability to construct effective written and oral communication appropriate to IT practitioners using skills including conflict resolution, contract negotiation, team formation, leadership, and team dynamics. | DL3 |
| 6 | Apply investigative research methods, models, and tools to IT professional practice; identify and critique changing information on the area of interest; and interpret the potential validity of results. | — |

## Unit Structure

This unit is delivered in-person with weekly 2-hour lectures and 1-hour tutorials.

Lectures are in-person on Thursdays from 5pm-7pm and will be held at: F19.02.201. Eastern Avenue Auditorium and Theatre Complex. Eastern Avenue. Lectures are recorded and will be available online the day after the presented lecture.

Tutorials start from Week 2, they are in-person only and are not recorded. Please check Allocate+ for your allocated tutorial session.

## Attendance

Lectures are not compulsory to attend and are recorded for students who have timetable clashes. It is strongly recommended that you attend lectures if you are able, as there will be (optional but useful) interactive tasks to complete during some lectures. Tutorials are in-person. You will be working in project teams within your timetable allocated tutorial and will miss out on content vital for this unit if you miss tutorials.

## Weekly Readings

There is no prescribed textbook for this unit. Instead, each week, there will be two sets of reading materials:

- **Foundations**: Each week, a set of short readings will be provided that cover the topics discussed in more detail in the lecture. It is expected that all students read and understand all of these foundation readings.
- **Modules**: Each week, there may be short reading(s) provided in the weekly module on canvas which would be relevant for that particular lecture and will cover topics discussed in more detail in the lecture. It is expected the students read this before attending the lecture.

You can find all the details on the Weekly Readings page.
