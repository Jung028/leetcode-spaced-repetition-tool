## Assessment Detail

- **Final Exam (50%)** — closed-book and supervised, held in the formal exam period. AI prohibited. Hurdle task.
- **Interactive Oral / Viva (10%)** — secured assessment, no notes allowed, held Week 8, covers Weeks 1–6 content, mandatory attendance. AI prohibited. Hurdle task.
- **Early Semester Feedback Task (5%)** — out-of-class, individual, auto-graded MCQ quiz, based on content from Week 1 to Week 3, due Week 4. AI allowed. Not a hurdle task.
- **Team Report / Group Assignment (35%)** — groups of 4–5 people, submission in Week 12. AI allowed. Not a hurdle task.

## Hurdle Requirement

Two of the four assessment items are hurdle tasks: the **Final Exam (50%)** and the **Interactive Oral / Viva (10%)**. Both are also the two assessments where AI use is prohibited. Failing to meet a hurdle requirement caps the outcome regardless of overall average (exact cap not stated in the Week 1 slides — confirm against the formal unit outline/Canvas policy page if needed).

## Viva Prep Notes

- Oral, no notes — content must be retained and recalled without written support, unlike the open-book Early Feedback Task.
- Covers Weeks 1–6 per the Week 1 Assessments slides, held Week 8 — a 2–7 week gap between learning the material and being examined on it, so early revision matters more than for a written exam sat closer to the material. **Note:** the Marking Criteria doc (below) says the Knowledge & Understanding criterion covers "Weeks 1–7" — this conflicts with the "Weeks 1–6" stated on the Assessments slides. Confirm which is authoritative against Canvas/the rubric release before finalising Week 6/7 content; until confirmed, treat coverage as Weeks 1–7 to be safe.

## Viva Marking Criteria

Source: INFO5990 Viva rubric (Canvas).

| Criterion | Points | Ratings (top → bottom) |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge & Understanding** | /40 | HD: excellent grasp of Weeks 1–7 concepts; integrates multiple ideas; depth beyond the answer guide. D: strong understanding, minor gaps, covers main ideas well. Good: adequate, mostly correct but sometimes lacks depth/clarity. Satisfactory: basic, limited/superficial, some inaccuracies. Fail: little/no understanding, frequent inaccuracies, cannot explain key concepts. |
| **Communication & Clarity** | /40 | HD (85–100%): confident, clear structure, logical flow, concise within 1–2 min/question. D (75–84%): clear, good structure, minor hesitation/wordiness. Satisfactory: understandable but occasionally unclear/disorganised, may go off-track. Minimal: hard to follow, frequent pauses/filler words, unclear flow. Fail: incoherent or incomplete, unable to sustain communication. |
| **Professionalism & Engagement** | /20 | HD (85–100%): highly professional, respectful, attentive, confident, strong engagement, academic integrity (no AI reliance). D (75–84%): professional, respectful, good engagement. Satisfactory: generally professional, some nervousness/disengagement. Minimal: basic professionalism, noticeable lack of confidence/engagement. Fail (<50%): unprofessional, dismissive, unprepared, or disengaged. |

Total: /100, weighted to 10% of the final mark.

### What this means for prep

- **Knowledge & Understanding (40 pts)** is not just "get the fact right" — top marks require *integrating multiple ideas* and going *beyond the answer guide*. Single-concept recall caps out around "Good"/"Distinction"; connect-the-dots synthesis across weeks/lectures is what separates Distinction from High Distinction.
- **Communication & Clarity (40 pts)** is scored independently of correctness — a right answer delivered as a rambling, unstructured 4-minute monologue loses marks here. Practice answering out loud, structured, within 1–2 minutes per question.
- **Professionalism & Engagement (20 pts)** rewards attentiveness, confidence, and *visible academic integrity (no AI reliance)* — practice should be about internalising the material for unaided recall, not producing notes to read from.

### What this means for the team report

- This is a **deliverable-quality rubric**, not a recall rubric — it doesn't map onto the SRS quiz/question-generation flow the way the Viva rubric does. Its value here is as a **drafting and self-review checklist**: before submission, check each of the 13 criteria against what's actually written, not just that the section exists (the rubric repeatedly distinguishes "topic is present" from "topic is analysed, evidenced, and project-specific" — most bands drop a level for being generic/templated rather than tailored to this org).
- Two criteria are easy to under-invest in relative to their weight: **Business challenge, governance and IT-enabled proposal (7 pts)** is the single highest-weighted criterion, and **SparkPlus peer evaluation and team participation (10 pts)** is graded on individual conduct/evaluations, independent of report quality — don't let report-writing effort crowd out completing both SparkPlus evaluations thoughtfully and on time.
- Recurring theme across top bands: "project-specific" / "tailored" beats "generic" at every criterion. A generic risk register, generic change plan, or generic governance structure caps out around "Approaches expectations" even if technically correct.
